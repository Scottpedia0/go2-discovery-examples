Three Rules

Before you start building anything

1
Start with your own tasks first Automate what you do every day before touching the team's workflows. Your daily work is the proving ground — if it saves you real time, then you bring it to your engagement leads.
2
Don't trust AI output without checking The verify skill uses a second AI model to catch mistakes. Use it. AI is bad at auditing its own work — the same way people are. Always run verification on anything that matters.

When you're stuck, just describe what you want You handle the what, Claude handles the how. If you can explain it to a new hire, you can explain it to this system.

Tips That Will Save You Time

Things Scott learned the hard way

Push back when it says it can't Claude will sometimes say "I can't do that." A lot of the time, it actually can — it just needs you to push. Try saying "yes you can, try it." Scott does this regularly and it works more often than you'd expect. Don't accept the first no.
Claude should help you through setup, not become the blocker If something is half-configured or locally broken, push it to solve the next setup step instead of giving you a speech about why it can't work. You know the work. Claude's job is to get you over the line.
Show, don't describe Instead of describing a problem, paste the actual text — the EOD entry, the Slack message, the error. Claude works better with real data than with summaries of data.
Ask it to save things If Claude builds you something useful, say "save that as [name].html" or "save that to a file." Otherwise it disappears when the conversation ends.
Say "show me a table" or "make it shorter" If the output is too long or not formatted the way you want, just tell it. "Put that in a table." "Just give me the names." "Make it one paragraph." It adjusts instantly.
Don't type a novel — talk in bullets Short messages work better than long paragraphs. "Check Emman, last 7 days, any flags" is better than explaining the whole context.

Your Built-In QA Partner

Something we engineered that you get for free

When you run something big — an audit, a report, a data pull — your Claude Code automatically deploys a second AI that watches in real time. We call it a session governor.

Think of it like a proofreader watching over your shoulder while you work. It catches problems as they happen, not after the whole thing is done.

Catches errors early If Claude queries the wrong table, misreads a number, or makes a claim the data doesn't support — the governor flags it before it compounds into the final output.
Prevents drift If Claude starts going off-track (answering a different question than what you asked), the governor pulls it back.
Keeps state If your session gets interrupted or loses context, the governor has a record of what was happening. Say "what was I working on?" and it resumes.
Challenges overconfidence If Claude says "I'm sure this is correct" without evidence, the governor pushes back. AI is bad at doubting itself — the governor does the doubting.

How it happened: Scott's team built this (internally called AIEA — AI Executive Assistant) after finding that AI errors compound over long sessions. A wrong table name in step 1 corrupts everything downstream. The governor is a cheaper second model (Sonnet) running alongside your main session that catches 80%+ of issues before they reach you. You don't need to do anything — it deploys automatically when you run data-heavy operations. If it doesn't, just say "deploy a session governor."

Session logs are saved to ~/Documents/go2-work/session-logs/ so you always have a record of what was done and can pick up where you left off.

Your Daily Intelligence Brief

Say "eod" at the end of your shift

When you're done for the day, type /eod or just say "end of day." Claude will:

Read your actual data Your meetings (with transcripts from Gemini auto-notes), your app usage from BigQuery, your team's SOD/EOD reports, your Claude Code conversations. Not a template — what actually happened today.
Compile action items you missed That meeting transcript you haven't had time to review? The action items are extracted. That EM whose reports have been declining? Flagged. That task you started but didn't finish? Carried forward.
Show you what you couldn't see The data reveals patterns you don't have time to notice when you're inside the work. Who's quietly struggling. Where your time actually went. What's getting better vs worse.
Email a summary to Scott A daily email goes to Scott (with you CC'd) summarizing what happened, your team's status, and what you got stuck on. He reads these. It's how he stays connected without needing to be in every conversation.

It asks you questions too. Not every day, but regularly. Things like: "What's the one problem on your mind that you don't think has a solution?" or "You spent 3 hours in meetings and 45 minutes on DEA work — is that the ratio you want?" These aren't scripted. They come from what actually happened in your day.

EOD reports are saved to ~/Documents/go2-work/eod-reports/ so you can look back at any day. Claude reads your last 3 EODs to track carry-forward items and spot trends.

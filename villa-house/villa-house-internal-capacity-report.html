Red Flags

1. Zero Redundancy

If Beng is out: JP absorbs orders, claims degrade. If Eric is out: JP absorbs some claims at 35% of Eric's throughput. If JP is out: the team splits into two islands with zero bridge. Beng cannot do claims. Eric cannot do orders. There is no fourth person. This is a 3-person team with the minimum viable staffing for a 2-function model.

2. Eric's Burnout Trajectory

Eric's caseload grew 76% in 6 months (199 to 351 cases/month). He is the only team member who ever rates feeling as "1" (3 times). He had consecutive "no win" days. His worklog hours grew from 85.8h to 141.0h per month. The system is extracting more from him every month. At some point, quality or attendance will break.

3. Workload Growth Without Headcount Change

Team hours grew 46% from Oct (309.8h) to Mar (451.8h). All three people are working more each month. This team lost a fourth member (Michelle Cayanan, June 2025) and never backfilled. Claims hours dropped 39% post-departure and the team has been slowly absorbing the gap. The absorption is now reaching limits.

4. EOD Reporting -- Beng Provides Zero Signal

Beng's EOD reports are identical every day: headline "Done [weekday]!", win "Same", concerns "Same", next shift "Same", feeling "3". 103 reports, zero variation. This is not necessarily a performance issue -- it may reflect a genuinely routine role -- but it means Go2's engagement coach has no visibility into Beng's actual state, workload pressure, or quality issues. The EOD system is capturing nothing useful from 33% of the team.

5. Meeting/Training Infrastructure Collapsed

Post-Michelle departure (June 2025): Meeting hours dropped 84% (4.5h/month to 0.7h). Training & Updates disappeared entirely. 1:1 with EM dropped 46%. The team stopped coordinating formally. There is no evidence of regular standups, team meetings, or structured knowledge sharing in the 6-month data window.

6. Zero Cross-Training

Beng and Eric have zero overlap in skills. Beng has never logged a claims hour. Eric has never logged an orders hour. In 6 months of 1,500+ combined hours, neither has touched the other's domain. If cross-training was ever planned post-Michelle, the data shows it never happened.

7. "Need Help" Flag Is Dead

Zero of 313 total EOD reports across all three people ever set "need_help" to yes. Either this team is genuinely self-sufficient, or the mechanism is socially inactive. Given Eric's documented workload pressure and sentiment variation, the latter seems more likely.

Capacity Planning Matrix

Can This Team Take On More Work?

No. All three people are at or above 99% tracked utilization. Monthly hours grew 46% in 6 months. There is zero slack on any person's calendar. The team cannot absorb additional workload without either degraded quality, extended hours (which are already growing), or someone burning out.

If Villa & House Grows 20%

What breaks first: Claims

Eric is already processing 16.1 cases/day at ~25 min each. A 20% increase means ~19.3 cases/day. At his current rate, that requires 8+ hours of pure case work, plus 2+ hours of overhead (Go2, Teams, breaks). He would need to work 10+ hour days or cut case processing time by 20%. The former is unsustainable; the latter risks quality.

What actually happens: JP gets pulled into claims more. He goes from 2 claims days/week to 3. Orders processing drops proportionally. Beng cannot absorb JP's orders -- she is at 99.8% utilization. Orders start backing up. Customer experience degrades on both the orders and claims sides simultaneously.

Rebalancing Options

Option A: Formalize JP's Split

JP's Mon-Wed EPACS / Thu-Fri Claims pattern is already organic. Formalize it and staff around it. This means accepting that orders have 3 people Mon-Wed and 2 on Thu-Fri, and claims have 2 people Thu-Fri and 1 Mon-Wed.

Risk: This cements the coverage gaps rather than fixing them.

Option B: Shift JP to Full-Time Claims, Hire EPACS Replacement

JP already handles claims competently (5.6 cases/day vs Eric's 16.1). With full-time focus, JP could potentially reach 10-12 cases/day. Hire a new person to take JP's EPACS duties alongside Beng.

Risk: Depends on new hire ramp time. Beng would need to train them.

Option C: Automate First, Then Reassess

The individual analyses identified ~96.5 hours/month of automatable work for Eric alone (template responses, carrier claim assembly, photo pipeline). Similar opportunities exist for Beng (shipping calculation, order data entry). Automation could reclaim 20-30% of each person's day, creating the slack that currently does not exist.

Risk: Implementation timeline. Automation does not help today.

The Bottom Line

For Frenchie: These three people are working hard, working honestly, and have no room left. The telemetry confirms it: 99%+ utilization, zero non-work activity, consistent attendance. The problem is not the people -- it is the math. A 3-person team lost its 4th member 10 months ago and workload has grown 46% since. They are absorbing it through longer hours and JP's dual-role flexibility, but that flexibility is now fully consumed. The next thing that goes wrong -- a sick week, a volume spike, a new client requirement -- will cause visible degradation. The engagement coach's priority should be (1) monitoring Eric for burnout, (2) pushing for cross-training between Beng and Eric, and (3) flagging to the client that this team is at maximum capacity with no buffer.
